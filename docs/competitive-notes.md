## Common patterns

1. **Discount first, care second.** All three chains open on a price or BOGOF banner, and specials multiply further down the page. Eye health comes after the deal.
2. **Phone numbers buried.** Chains put the number in the footer. Two of the independents don't show it on the homepage at all. For older patients and for "can you see me today?" calls, this is the most important missing detail.
3. **Booking friction.** Booking means a nav link to a separate system, or an email-style form with no live slots. None of the homepages shows the next available time or says how long the visit takes.
4. **Medical aid is an afterthought or absent.** In KZN, "do you take my medical aid / what will it cover?" is the main question patients ask. The chains tuck it into secondary navigation or a single partner promo. The independents we reviewed don't mention it.
5. **Cluttered grids and carousels.** Six-card "Learn more" service grids, multi-slide carousels, rows of brand logos and nested menus everywhere. Every card is the same size, so nothing stands out.
6. **Generic imagery.** Stock clinic photos and product shots. The chains have little local or human imagery, and none of these sites says anything about KZN or its communities.
7. **Weak independent web presence.** Wix templates, sites in maintenance mode, PHP errors, Facebook pages standing in for websites. Independents lose to the chains on polish even though their care is more personal.

## Gaps we exploit

- **Calm over clamour.** One message per screen and no promo banners. Pricing is transparent and shown in context, never as a shouty BOGOF.
- **One-tap booking, everywhere.** A persistent "Book" button that opens live slots (next available time shown up front), plus tap-to-call and WhatsApp in a sticky bar on mobile. The phone number is never more than a thumb away.
- **Medical aid up front.** A clear "we claim directly from your medical aid" block that names the common KZN schemes and explains what an eye test usually costs out of pocket. It answers the main question before the patient has to ask.
- **Cinematic, local imagery.** Iris macro and slow-scroll storytelling instead of carousels. A blurry-to-sharp Durban beachfront slider that shows what correction does. Real, varied KZN faces (Black, Indian, coloured and white families) and local landmarks (Moses Mabhida, the Drakensberg) instead of generic stock.
- **Care before commerce.** Lead with the exam itself (eye health, glaucoma and diabetic screening, kids' eyes), then frames, shown as a few curated pairs rather than a catalogue grid.
- **Fast, modern build.** A single performant site that clearly outclasses the Wix and legacy-PHP independents and feels more personal than the chains.
