# Iris: a cinematic optometrist website (KZN demo)

> **The page is an eye, and scrolling is how you look inside it.**

This is a production-grade demo site for a fictional optometry practice in Umhlanga, KwaZulu-Natal. A procedural 3D human eye sits behind the page. As you scroll, the camera goes through the cornea and the iris opens like an aperture. The lens floats free and brings an eye chart into focus. You then travel through the vitreous to a glowing retina, and out along the optic nerve into the warm, human side of the practice. It ends at a one-minute booking card, with the eye reassembled in miniature beside it.

Everything is server-rendered semantic HTML. The 3D is a progressive enhancement, so services, care, contact and booking are all readable and usable without it.

## Quick start

```bash
# Node 20+ and pnpm 9+ (corepack enable pnpm)
pnpm install
pnpm dev            # http://localhost:3000
```

| `pnpm build`     | Production build (type-checks and lints)                       |

### Deploy (Vercel, zero config)

1. Push the repo to GitHub, GitLab or Bitbucket.
2. In Vercel, choose **Add New → Project**, import the repo and keep the defaults. Vercel detects Next.js and pnpm.
3. Set `practice.url` in `src/config/practice.ts` to the live domain. It is used for canonical URLs, Open Graph, the sitemap and JSON-LD.

There are no environment variables, no database and no CMS.

### The eye (procedural, no model files)

Every part is its own mesh, built in code:

- **Sclera:** canvas-generated vessel texture, wet clearcoat and a warm sheen at grazing angles.
- **Cornea:** transmission dome (IOR 1.376) that refracts the iris and reflects a window-shaped light. A reflection-only fallback is used on the Low tier.
- **Iris:** a GLSL shader with radial fibres, crypts, collarette, contraction furrows, limbal ring and a slow shimmer. It is built from 12 aperture blades that peel open in the vertex shader.
- **Pupil:** reacts to your pointer, which acts as the light source. It constricts fast and dilates slowly, as a real pupil does, with a faint physiological hippus.
- **Lens:** biconvex lathe. Its transmission roughness _is_ the focus pull, and it squashes as the focus changes (accommodation).
- **Vitreous:** drifting floaters.
- **Retina:** a procedurally grown vascular tree, optic disc and macula. Light sweeps out from the disc, and pulses travel along the vessels.
- **Optic nerve:** tapered sheath with fibre strands carrying signal pulses away from the eye.
- **Lids:** randomised natural blinking, including occasional double blinks.

The eye also tracks the cursor, uses device tilt on phones where allowed, and drifts idly otherwise.

### v2 design language: "the exam room"

The second pass replaced the generic premium-template look with a visual language borrowed from real optometry instruments:

- **Type.** Bodoni Moda headlines with italic emphasis. Write `*word*` in `practice.ts` and it renders as gold italic on dark scenes. Body text is Bricolage Grotesque. Instrument-style read-outs and labels use Martian Mono.
- **Focus-pull headlines.** Every headline arrives out of focus, enlarged and split into a red/cyan chromatic fringe like a misaligned lens, then converges from the centre outwards (`src/lib/textFx.ts`, GSAP SplitText). It is scrubbed in the story and triggered on view in the content sections. The hero does the same in pure CSS so LCP isn't delayed.
- **Anatomy read-outs.** Each scene shows a real measurement (cornea 0.55 mm and 43 D, lens +20 D, 126 million photoreceptors…) that decodes like an instrument settling (ScrambleText).
- **Depth gauge.** The side meter is a ruler reading your real depth into the eye in millimetres. It shows the long empty gap of the vitreous, as real anatomy has.
- **Trial lenses.** Services are drawn as lenses from an optometrist's trial kit: steel rim, tinted glass and a stamped power on the handle. They drop and spin into place as the iris opens.
- **Iris aperture.** The 12 blades swing open on pins at the rim in a clock-wipe sequence, like a camera iris.
- **The page blinks.** Two eyelids sweep closed over the whole page between chapters. On the last blink they glow warm red, like daylight through closed lids, before opening onto the care section.
- **Retinal scan.** A cyan laser line sweeps the fundus with an imaging grid. Labels for the optic disc, macula and vessel arcade are pinned to the actual 3D points.
- **Care section.**
  - The Care Promise is an Rx prescription slip (OD, OS, OU) with a signature that draws itself and a practice stamp.
  - Testimonials are lined patient notes.
  - Medical aids are set as an eye chart with acuity labels.
  - The booking form is a perforated appointment slip with round "lens" day selectors.
- **Footer.** It is the bottom of the eye chart: "See _everything._" huge, lines shrinking down to "If you can read this, you might still be due an eye test."
- **Details.**
  - A reticle cursor turns into a dilating iris over anything clickable.
  - Primary buttons have a mini iris that dilates on hover, and their labels refocus.
  - Viewfinder focus brackets frame the photos and the hero HUD.
  - Frame photos carry real frame-size notation (49□21 145).
- **The eye.**
  - A three-stop hazel iris (gold collarette, brown body, sea-green rim) with gold flecks. Brown and hazel is the most common iris colour in KZN.
  - Natural almond lids with a contact-shadow skirt.
  - A ring-light catchlight.
  - The hero posters are fresh renders of the new eye: landscape for desktop, portrait for phones.

### Scroll choreography

There is one master GSAP timeline, authored in _scene time_ t ∈ [0, 7]. Scroll maps to `t` through DOM anchors (`[data-t]`), so the 3D stays in sync whatever height a section ends up on a given phone. Lenis smooths the scroll and shares GSAP's ticker. **`docs/motion-spec.md` is the contract.** It has a scene-by-scene table of ranges, camera positions, uniforms and copy, plus the boot policy.

### Two signature moments

1. **Focus pull** (Lens scene): a real-text Snellen chart sharpens line by line as you scroll. The dioptre readout counts −2.75 D → 0.00 D, and the 3D lens's own blur resolves in sync, with letters behind it refracting into focus.
2. **See the difference:** drag across real KZN views (Durban beachfront, Drakensberg, Moses Mabhida) to correct short-sightedness, astigmatism (ghosted double edges) or a cloudy lens (haze and glare). It's keyboard accessible (`role="slider"`).

Always-on extras: the pointer is the light (the pupil reacts), a pupil-style cursor dilates over interactive elements and constricts over the 3D, the depth meter is clickable, the sticky nav hides on scroll down, the CTAs are magnetic, numbers count up, and leader lines draw in.

## Performance, devices and accessibility

**Boot policy.** First paint is server HTML plus a poster that is a render of the same eye. The hero text reveal is pure CSS. GSAP, Lenis and the timeline are imported after hydration. The 3D chunk loads on first intent: on desktop that's a pointer move, wheel or key, or 3.5s idle. On touch devices and Save-Data connections it loads only when the visitor engages, so capped data stays capped. Shaders compile asynchronously before the first frame.

**Quality tiers** (auto-detected from cores, memory, Save-Data and pointer type, with a manual toggle in the footer):

| Tier   | DPR | Post                     | Notes                                                     |
| ------ | --- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| High   | ≤ 2 | Bloom, CA, DOF, vignette | 2K procedural textures, transmission                      |
| Medium | 1.5 | Bloom, CA, vignette      | Default for phones and tablets                            |
| Low    | 1   | None (CSS vignette)      | Simplified shaders, no transmission, Lightformer env only |

`PerformanceMonitor` only lowers resolution, and drops post as a last resort. It never rebuilds shaders mid-scroll. Rendering pauses while the opaque Care section covers the canvas.

**Fallbacks.**

- `prefers-reduced-motion`: a calm static layout with posters and simple fades. The eye still renders in the hero, idle, with no camera moves.
- No WebGL, a software renderer (SwiftShader/llvmpipe), or a WebGL error: the full static site with imagery. Add `?force3d` to override this for demos on unusual hardware.

**Lighthouse** (production build, local, headless Chrome, median of 3 runs):

| Mode                       | Performance | Accessibility | Best Practices | SEO |
| -------------------------- | ----------- | ------------- | -------------- | --- |
| Mobile (simulated slow 4G) | 88–89       | 100           | 100            | 100 |
| Desktop                    | 99–100      | 100           | 100            | 100 |

*v2, local Windows build, with the Next.js font-preload bug below unpatched. v1 scored 88–89 under the same patched conditions. v2 pays for its richer typography and motion: split-text headlines, the Rx card and eye charts add DOM, and there is ~450 ms more main-thread work at 4× CPU. Observed (unthrottled) FCP/LCP is ~1.0 s. First-load JS is 125 kB; three.js is a separate lazy chunk.

**Accessibility.**

- Semantic landmarks and headings, plus a skip link.
- Visible focus rings and 44px+ tap targets.
- An ARIA depth meter (`aria-current="step"`).
- A carousel with `aria-roledescription` and a live region.
- Form errors announced via `role="alert"`, with focus moved to the first error.
- AA text contrast.
- The 3D canvas is `pointer-events: none` and `aria-hidden`, so it never traps scroll or blocks links or text selection.

**SEO.**

- Metadata and an Open Graph image generated from config.
- `Optician` (LocalBusiness) JSON-LD with hours, geo, services and areas served.
- `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt`.

**POPIA and privacy.**

- No tracking scripts or cookies.
- The booking form says what the data is used for and asks for consent.
- The map is a zero-request illustration until the visitor asks for the interactive Google map.

## Booking

`POST /api/book` validates with the **same Zod schema** as the form (`src/lib/booking.ts`). It checks for a South African number and an open day, requires consent, and includes a honeypot. For now it **logs the request and returns success**.

## Decisions, placeholders and known issues

- **Placeholders to replace per client** (all in `src/config/practice.ts`): address, phone and WhatsApp numbers, email, team names and HPCSA number, social links, stats (including the 4.9★ rating), and the **sample testimonials**. These are flagged `testimonialsAreSample: true` and labelled "(sample)" on the page.
- **Medical aids** are typeset as names, not logos, to respect trademarks. The copy says "we assist with claims".
- **Display font:** Bodoni Moda (OFL), a fashion-eyewear didone. `src/fonts/` holds a custom static instance pinned at its display optical size (opsz 96, regular weight) and subset to Latin with fontTools. That keeps the full hairline contrast at ~27 KB for roman and italic, against ~100 KB for the variable Google files. To re-cut it, see the comment in `src/app/layout.tsx`.
- **Images:** all 16 photos were fetched, checked and credited (Unsplash/Pexels). Nothing is missing, so no placeholders are needed. See `docs/image-credits.md`. The hero poster is a render of the site's own eye.
- **HDRI:** Poly Haven _Studio Small 09_ (CC0), downscaled to 512×256 (377 KB) and only loaded on High/Medium.
- **Windows-only build quirk (Next.js 15.5):** the `next-font-manifest` plugin matches `'/next-font-loader/index.js?'` with forward slashes. On Windows, font preload `<link>`s and size-adjusted fallbacks are therefore silently dropped from local builds. **Vercel and Linux builds are unaffected.** The Lighthouse numbers above were measured with that line patched locally to mirror Linux behaviour; the repo itself is unpatched.
- `?debug` exposes `window.__iris` (story state and quality store) for inspection. It is also on automatically in development.

## How it was verified

The site was checked at 360, 390, 768 and 1440px widths, in cinematic, reduced-motion and no-WebGL modes. Scripted headless Chrome (puppeteer-core) captured screenshots at each scene time `t`. The same scripts tested the booking flow end to end (validation, focus management, success state), the mobile menu, and the API's valid and invalid payloads. Lint, typecheck and the production build all pass with zero errors.
